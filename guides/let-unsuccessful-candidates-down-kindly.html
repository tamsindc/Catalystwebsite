When to use it

When you've made a hiring decision

Step by step

  1. Write down why, against your criteria

    For anyone who interviewed, note one or two job-related reasons from your scoring sheet, such as 'less experience of managing budgets than other candidates'.

  2. Ask AI for the messages

    Paste this prompt into your AI assistant.

    Prompt to copyWrite 3 rejection email templates for [job title] at [business]: 1) applied but not shortlisted (under 80 words), 2) interviewed, no detailed feedback (under 100 words), 3) interviewed, with brief feedback (under 140 words) using these points: [job-related points from scoring sheet]. All should thank them sincerely, be clear the decision is final, avoid any comments on personality or anything not related to the job criteria, and wish them well. Template 3 should frame feedback constructively. British English, warm, human. Use [Name] placeholders.

  3. Personalise and send promptly

    Add one specific, genuine line where you can, such as something they did well. Send within a few days of deciding.

What you get

Hi [Name], thank you for coming in to meet us on Tuesday and for preparing so thoroughly. We enjoyed hearing about your work at the farm shop. We've decided to offer the role to someone with more experience of running stock systems. We'd be happy to hear from you for future roles. Wishing you all the best.

Tips

  • Keep feedback tied to the job criteria; it's fairer and protects you if a decision is ever questioned.
  • Tell candidates how long you'll keep their details, in line with your privacy notice.
