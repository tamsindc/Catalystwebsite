Compare products side by side before you buy

Get a clear comparison table of the products you're considering, covering price, features, common complaints and which suits your needs.

When to use it

Before any purchase over about £100

Step by step

  1. Write down what you need

    List your budget, must-haves and deal-breakers. For example: 'under £400, quiet, fits a 60cm gap'.

  2. Ask for a comparison table

    Use an AI assistant with web search on, and paste this prompt with your shortlist (or ask it to suggest one).

    Prompt to copyI'm choosing a [product type]. My budget is [amount], I must have [must-haves] and I want to avoid [deal-breakers]. Compare these options: [product 1], [product 2], [product 3] (or suggest 3 if I haven't listed any). Make a table with: price range, key specs, warranty, what owners praise, what owners complain about most, and a link to the manufacturer's page. Then tell me which fits my needs best and why. Say where information was hard to find or might be out of date.

  3. Check price and specs at the source

    Prices and model numbers change often. Confirm the details on the manufacturer's or retailer's page before you buy.

  4. Read a few real reviews

    Skim three or four recent owner reviews of your top choice to make sure the complaints the AI found are real and still relevant.
