Stress-test your argument before a hearing

Hear the strongest points the other side is likely to make, the weak spots in your own case and questions a judge may ask, so nothing surprises you.

LawyerBeginner15 minAI assistantWord
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

A few days before a hearing or meeting

Step by step

  1. Summarise your case briefly

    Write a one-page summary of the facts, the issues and your main arguments. Leave out names and anything confidential unless your AI tool is firm-approved.

  2. Ask it to argue the other side

    Paste the summary with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyHere is a summary of my argument in a [type of case] where I act for the [party]. Play the role of experienced opposing counsel. 1) Give the 5 strongest counter-arguments, most dangerous first. 2) For each, say which fact or gap in my case it exploits. 3) List 5 questions a judge might ask me. 4) Suggest what further evidence or points would answer each weakness. Base this on the facts given. If you mention any case or statute, label it 'unverified' so I can check it. Summary: [paste]

  3. Verify anything legal it mentions

    AI can invent case names and citations that look real. Treat every authority it mentions as unverified until you have read it in a proper legal source.

  4. Update your plan

    Add answers to the strongest points to your notes, and discuss any serious weaknesses with colleagues or the client as you normally would.

What you get

1. Notice was never given in writing, so the clause wasn't triggered. (Exploits: no letter on file.) 2. Your client kept paying rent, which suggests they accepted the state of the property. Judge may ask: 'Why did your client wait four months to complain?'

Tips

  • Ask for a second round: 'Now reply to each counter-argument as if you were me' to test your answers.
  • Use it for preparation only. Your professional judgment decides the strategy.
