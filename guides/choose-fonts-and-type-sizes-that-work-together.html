Choose fonts and type sizes that work together

Describe the project and get three font pairings with suggested sizes for headings, body and captions, and the reason each one suits the job.

Graphic designerBeginner5 minAI assistantCanva

When to use it

When starting the layout for a new piece

Step by step

  1. Note what the piece needs to do

    Write down the format, the audience, the mood and any brand fonts you must keep. Mention whether it is for print or screen.

  2. Ask for pairings and a type scale

    Paste this into your AI assistant and fill in the brackets.

    Prompt to copyI'm designing a [format, e.g. A5 leaflet] for [audience] that should feel [mood words]. It will be seen [in print / on screen at size]. Fonts I must use: [brand fonts or 'none']. Suggest 3 font pairings using free fonts available in Google Fonts or Canva. For each give: heading font and weight, body font and weight, why they work together, a size and line spacing for main heading, subheading, body text and captions, and one thing to avoid. Present it as a table, then tell me which you'd pick and why.

  3. Try them side by side

    Set up the same short block of real copy three times in Canva or your design app using each pairing. Look at them at actual size, printed if it is going to print.

  4. Save the winner as a style

    Once you choose, save the fonts and sizes as text styles or a brand kit so the rest of the piece stays consistent.

What you get

Pairing 1: Playfair Display Bold headings with Source Sans 3 Regular body. Why: classic contrast, easy to read small. Sizes for A5: heading 28pt, subheading 14pt, body 10pt with 14pt spacing, captions 8pt. Avoid: setting Playfair in long paragraphs.

Tips

  • Always check the font's licence covers your client's use, especially for logos and paid products.
  • Ask the AI for a version with larger body text if the audience includes older readers.
