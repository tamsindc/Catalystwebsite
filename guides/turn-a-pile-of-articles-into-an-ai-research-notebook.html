Turn a pile of articles into an AI research notebook

Load your articles, PDFs and links into NotebookLM and ask questions answered only from your sources, with citations you can click.

Research & NewsBeginner15 minNotebookLMGoogle Drive
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

When you're researching one topic over several weeks

Step by step

  1. Gather your sources

    Collect the articles, reports and PDFs you trust on the topic. Save PDFs to a Google Drive folder so they're in one place.

  2. Create a notebook

    Open NotebookLM (free with a Google account), create a new notebook and add your sources: upload files, pick them from Drive or paste website links.

  3. Ask questions from your sources

    Type questions in the chat. Answers come with numbered citations that jump to the exact passage. Try this to start.

    Prompt to copyUsing only these sources, give me: 1) the 5 main themes across them, 2) where the sources agree and disagree, 3) the key figures or statistics with which source each comes from, 4) the questions these sources don't answer about [my specific question].

  4. Click the citations

    Check a few citations to make sure the answer matches the passage. It sticks to your sources, but it can still misread them.

  5. Save useful answers

    Save good answers as notes in the notebook so your findings build up as you go.

What you get

Themes: 1) four-day week trials report lower sick leave [1][3]; 2) mixed effects on customer response times [2]... Disagreement: [1] reports productivity up; [4] found no change. Not covered: effects on very small teams under 5 people.

Tips

  • Only upload documents you're allowed to share with an online tool; keep confidential work files out unless your workplace approves it.
