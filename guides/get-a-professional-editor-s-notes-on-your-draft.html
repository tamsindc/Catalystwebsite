Get a professional editor's notes on your draft

Line-by-line feedback on clarity, structure and tone for anything you've written, plus a tidied version you can accept or ignore.

When to use it

Before publishing or sending anything important

Step by step

  1. Paste your draft

    Copy your finished draft into your AI assistant. Tell it who will read it and what you want them to do afterwards.

  2. Ask for edits, not a rewrite

    Use this prompt so your voice stays intact.

    Prompt to copyAct as a careful editor. Reader: [who]. Goal: [what they should think or do]. Draft: [paste]. Don't rewrite it in your voice. Give me: 1) the 3 biggest problems (structure, clarity, missing information); 2) a list of specific line edits as 'Original -> Suggested -> Why'; 3) any sentences over 30 words to split; 4) jargon to replace; 5) a one-line verdict on whether the opening makes people want to keep reading. Use British English spelling.

  3. Accept what helps

    Make the changes you agree with in Google Docs. Ignore anything that flattens your style; you're the author.

What you get

Big problem 1: Your main point appears in paragraph 4; move it to the top. Line edit: 'In order to facilitate' -> 'To help' (simpler). Long sentence: para 2, 42 words. Verdict: The opening is a definition; start with the customer story instead.

Tips

  • Run a second pass asking only for spelling, grammar and consistency once you're happy with the structure.
