Protect time for focused work every week

Set up recurring focus blocks in your calendar that decline clashing meetings, sized and placed to suit how you work.

Meetings & CalendarBeginner10 minGoogle CalendarOutlook CalendarAI assistant
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

Set up once, review monthly

Step by step

  1. Work out your best hours

    Ask your AI assistant to help you decide where focus time should go.

    Prompt to copyHelp me plan weekly focus time. My usual meetings: [e.g. Mon 10am team, Wed 2pm 1:1s]. My best concentration time: [e.g. mornings]. Work I need deep focus for: [tasks]. Suggest 3 to 4 recurring focus blocks per week, each 90 minutes to 2 hours, avoiding my meetings. Give each a clear name that tells colleagues what I'm doing, and a one-line note for the event description explaining how to reach me if it's urgent.

  2. Create the blocks

    In Google Calendar, choose 'Focus time' as the event type if your account has it, which can auto-decline meetings. In Outlook, create a recurring event marked as Busy, or use Viva Insights focus time if your plan includes it.

  3. Silence notifications

    Turn on Do Not Disturb on your phone and computer during the blocks, and pause chat notifications in Slack or Teams.

What you get

Tue 9:00-11:00 Focus: quarterly report. Thu 9:00-11:00 Focus: client proposals. Fri 13:30-15:00 Focus: planning next week. Description: 'Heads-down time. For anything urgent, call me on my mobile.'

Tips

  • Treat focus blocks like a meeting with your most important client: move them, don't delete them.
