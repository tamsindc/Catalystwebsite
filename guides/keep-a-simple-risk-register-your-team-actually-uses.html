Keep a simple risk register your team actually uses

Build a shared risk register with clear ratings, owners and controls, and get a short monthly summary of what's changed.

When to use it

Set up once, review monthly

Step by step

  1. Brainstorm the risks

    Describe your department to your AI assistant and use this prompt to get a starting list.

    Prompt to copyI manage the [finance department / branch] of a [type and size of organisation]. Our main activities: [list]. Draft a starting risk register as a table: Ref | Risk (cause, event, effect) | Category (financial, operational, people, fraud, compliance, systems) | Likelihood 1-5 | Impact 1-5 | Score | Existing controls | Further action | Owner (role). Include about 15 risks. Use a consistent 1-5 scale and explain the scale in a short key. These are suggestions for me to review, not a final assessment.

  2. Make it yours

    Go through the list with your team, delete what doesn't apply, add what's missing and set the ratings yourselves. The AI's ratings are just a starting point.

  3. Share it

    Save the register in Excel in your team's Teams channel so owners can update their own rows.

  4. Review monthly

    Book a recurring monthly review in Outlook Calendar. Before it, paste the register into your AI assistant and ask: 'Compare with last month's version: list new risks, changed scores, overdue actions and the top five by score.'

What you get

R07 | Payments made to a fraudulent supplier bank account after a fake change-of-details email | Fraud | 3 | 5 | 15 | Call-back check on known number | Add second approver for any bank detail change | Finance manager. Top movers this month: R03 key person dependency up from 9 to 16.

Tips

  • Write each risk as 'cause, event, effect'; it makes the controls much easier to choose.
  • Don't paste client personal data, full account numbers or payroll details into a public AI tool unless your organisation allows it. Use your work-approved assistant if you have one.
