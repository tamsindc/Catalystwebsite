Get the right spreadsheet formula by describing it

Describe what you want in everyday words and get a working Excel or Google Sheets formula, explained so you understand what it does.

Documents & DataBeginner3 minAI assistantExcelGoogle Sheets
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

Whenever you're stuck on a formula

Step by step

  1. Note where your data is

    Write down which columns hold what, and which row your data starts on. For example: 'Names in A, amounts in C, from row 2'.

  2. Describe what you want

    Paste this prompt into your AI assistant.

    Prompt to copyI'm using [Excel / Google Sheets]. My data: [column letters and what's in each], starting on row [2]. I want a formula in cell [cell] that [describe the result, e.g. adds up column C only where column B says 'Paid' and the date in column D is this month]. Give me: the formula, a plain-English explanation of each part, and what to change if my data grows. Also tell me one common mistake to avoid.

  3. Test it on a few rows

    Paste the formula in and check the result against a few rows you can work out in your head. If it errors, copy the error message back to the assistant.

What you get

=SUMIFS(C:C, B:B, "Paid", D:D, ">="&EOMONTH(TODAY(),-1)+1, D:D, "<="&EOMONTH(TODAY(),0)) It adds column C where B is 'Paid' and the date in D falls in the current month. Mistake to avoid: dates stored as text won't be counted.

Tips

  • It works the other way round too: paste a formula you inherited and ask what it does.
