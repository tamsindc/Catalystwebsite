Reach out to people for career advice

Short, friendly messages to old colleagues or people in a field you're curious about, asking for a quick chat without feeling awkward.

CareersBeginner10 minAI assistantLinkedIn
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

When you want advice or a foot in the door

Step by step

  1. Pick three people

    Choose a mix: someone you know well, a former colleague you've lost touch with, and someone doing a job you're curious about.

  2. Draft the messages

    Paste a few details about each person into your AI assistant with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyWrite short LinkedIn messages (under 80 words each) for these people. My situation: [for example exploring a move into project management]. Person 1: [how I know them, what they do]. Person 2: [...]. Person 3: [...]. Each message should be warm and specific, mention why I'm contacting them in particular, ask for a 15-minute chat or one piece of advice, and make it easy to say no. No flattery, no asking for a job directly. Then write a short follow-up for anyone who doesn't reply within 2 weeks.

  3. Personalise and send

    Edit each message so it sounds like you, then send through LinkedIn or email.

  4. Prepare for the chat

    If someone says yes, ask the AI for 5 good questions to ask about their role and how they got into it.

What you get

Hi Jen, it's been ages since our days on the Riverside reception desk! I saw you're now a project coordinator at a housing charity. I'm thinking about a similar move and would love 15 minutes to hear how you made the jump. Completely understand if you're too busy.

Tips

  • Most people are glad to help when the ask is small and specific.
  • Always thank people afterwards and let them know what you did with their advice.
