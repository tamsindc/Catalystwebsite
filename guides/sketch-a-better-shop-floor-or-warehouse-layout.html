Sketch a better shop floor or warehouse layout

Two or three layout options for your space with the pros and cons of each, based on how people and goods actually move around.

Operations managerBeginner20 minAI assistantPhone camera
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

Before a refit, a seasonal change or when the space feels cramped

Step by step

  1. Capture the current space

    Take photos from each corner and draw a rough floor plan with measurements, doors, tills, fire exits and anything that can't move.

  2. Note what isn't working

    List the problems you see, such as queues blocking aisles, long walks to pick popular items or bottlenecks at the door.

  3. Ask for layout options

    Attach the photos and plan to your AI assistant with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyI manage a [shop / warehouse / workspace] of about [size]. Attached are photos and a rough floor plan. Fixed items: [doors, fire exits, tills, pillars]. Problems now: [list]. Goals: [e.g. faster picking, better flow for customers, more space for best-sellers]. Suggest three layout options. For each: describe where everything goes in plain words, the main benefit, the main drawback, rough effort to change, and anything to check for safety or accessibility (wide enough aisles, clear exits). Then recommend one and say why.

  4. Check safety and test it

    Run the preferred option past your health and safety lead, then mark it out with tape or move one area first to see if it works.

What you get

Option B: Move best-sellers to the two bays nearest dispatch and slow movers to the back. Benefit: shorter picking walks. Drawback: one weekend to move stock. Check: keep the fire exit aisle clear and at least the required width.

Tips

  • Ask staff who work the floor every day for their problems first; they'll spot things the photos won't show.
