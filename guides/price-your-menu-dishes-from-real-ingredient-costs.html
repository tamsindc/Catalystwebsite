Price your menu dishes from real ingredient costs

Work out what each plate costs to make and get a suggested selling price that hits your target food cost percentage.

Head chefBeginner20 minAI assistantGoogle Sheets
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

When writing a new menu or after supplier price changes

Step by step

  1. Gather recipes and prices

    For each dish, list ingredients with the quantity per portion. Then note the latest price and pack size for each ingredient from your supplier invoices.

  2. Ask for a costing table

    Paste both into your AI assistant with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyCost these dishes for a restaurant. Recipes with quantities per portion: [paste]. Ingredient prices and pack sizes: [paste]. Allow [5]% for waste and trim unless I say otherwise. For each dish give a table: ingredient, quantity, cost per portion; then total plate cost. Then suggest a menu price including [20]% VAT that gives a food cost of [28-30]%, rounded to a sensible menu price. Finish with a summary table of all dishes: plate cost, suggested price, food cost %. Show calculations so I can check them.

  3. Check and put it in a sheet

    Paste the tables into Google Sheets, spot-check a few lines against your invoices and adjust prices with your knowledge of what your customers will pay.

  4. Update when prices move

    When a supplier price changes, update the ingredient line and ask the AI which dishes have drifted above your target.

What you get

Pan-roast chicken, mash, greens: plate cost 4.12 (chicken supreme 2.35, butter 0.38...). At 30% food cost the price is 16.50 incl. VAT; suggested menu price 16.50. Hake special: plate cost 5.60, food cost 33% at 17.00 - consider 18.50.

Tips

  • Labour and overheads matter too; ask your accountant what overall margin you need before setting final prices.
