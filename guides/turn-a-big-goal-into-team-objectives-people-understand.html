Turn a big goal into team objectives people understand

Clear, measurable objectives for each team, linked to one company goal, with what success looks like and how you'll check progress.

Operations managerBeginner20 minAI assistantGoogle Docs
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

At the start of a quarter or year

Step by step

  1. Write down the goal and the teams

    Note the goal from leadership, the teams who contribute to it, and any limits on budget or people.

  2. Ask for draft objectives

    Paste this into your AI assistant.

    Prompt to copyI'm an operations manager. Our goal for [period] is: [goal]. The teams involved are: [list teams and what each does]. Constraints: [budget, headcount, deadlines]. For each team, suggest two or three objectives that clearly link to the goal. For each objective give: a plain one-sentence statement, a measure and target, how we'll track it, who owns it, and one risk that could knock it off course. Finish with a one-paragraph explanation of the goal I could read out to all staff, avoiding jargon.

  3. Agree them with team leads

    Use the draft to start the conversation, not end it. Ask each team lead what's realistic and change the targets together.

  4. Share and track

    Put the agreed objectives in a shared Google Doc and review progress at each monthly meeting.

What you get

Goal: cut late deliveries to customers. Warehouse: pick 98% of orders by 2pm (tracked daily in the dispatch sheet, owner: Warehouse lead). Risk: peak season absence. Customer service: tell customers about delays within 1 hour...

Tips

  • Fewer objectives work better; if a team has more than three, ask the assistant which ones matter most for the goal.
