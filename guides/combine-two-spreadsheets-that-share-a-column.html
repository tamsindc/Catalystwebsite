Combine two spreadsheets that share a column

Match up two lists, such as customers and orders, by a shared column, with a formula you understand and a check for anything that didn't match.

Documents & DataIntermediate20 minExcelGoogle SheetsAI assistant
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

When information you need is split across two files

Step by step

  1. Put both lists in one file

    Copy each list into its own tab in the same spreadsheet. Make sure both have a column that matches, such as an email or order number.

  2. Describe the match

    Paste this prompt into your AI assistant, along with the header rows of each tab.

    Prompt to copyI'm using [Excel / Google Sheets]. Tab '[tab 1 name]' has columns: [list]. Tab '[tab 2 name]' has columns: [list]. Both share [matching column]. I want to add [column(s) to bring across] from [tab 2] into [tab 1]. Give me: the formula (use XLOOKUP if available, otherwise VLOOKUP or INDEX/MATCH), which cell to put it in, how to fill it down, how to show 'Not found' instead of an error, and how to count how many rows didn't match. Explain each step simply.

  3. Fill it down and check

    Add the formula, fill it down, then filter for 'Not found'. These are usually spelling differences or extra spaces in the matching column.

  4. Fix the mismatches

    Tidy the matching column (for example with TRIM or by fixing typos) and the formula will update automatically.

What you get

In Customers!E2: =XLOOKUP(B2, Orders!A:A, Orders!D:D, "Not found"). Fill down to row 540. Count of unmatched: =COUNTIF(E:E, "Not found") gives 17. Most were emails with trailing spaces or typos.

Tips

  • Emails and codes are better matching columns than names, which are often spelled differently.
