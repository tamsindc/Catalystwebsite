Build a revision timetable you can actually stick to

A realistic week-by-week revision plan that fits around school, work and family, added straight into your calendar.

LearningIntermediate25 minAI assistantGoogle Calendar
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

Six to eight weeks before exams

Step by step

  1. List your exams and topics

    Write down each subject, exam date, the topics in it, and how confident you feel on each from 1 to 5.

  2. Note your available time

    Write down roughly when you're free each day, and any days that are off-limits, such as sports nights or shifts.

  3. Ask for a plan

    Paste everything into your AI assistant with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyMake me a revision timetable from [start date] to my last exam. Exams and dates: [list]. Topics and my confidence (1 to 5): [list]. My free time: [describe]. Rules: sessions of [30-45] minutes with short breaks, no more than [3] hours a day, a day off each week, more time on low-confidence topics, and revisit each topic at least twice with growing gaps. Finish each week with a practice paper. Give me a table with: Date, Time, Subject, Topic, Activity (for example flashcards, past paper, summary sheet).

  4. Put it in your calendar

    Add the sessions to Google Calendar as events. You can ask the AI to format them as a list of titles and times to make this quicker.

  5. Review on Sundays

    Each Sunday, tell the AI what you managed and what slipped, and ask it to adjust the next week.

What you get

Mon 18 Nov 4:30-5:15pm | Maths | Quadratics (confidence 2) | Worked examples + 5 practice questions Mon 5:30-6:15pm | History | Causes of WW1 | Make a mind map Sat 23 Nov 10-11:30am | Maths | Practice paper 1 | Timed

Tips

  • Plan for less than you think you can do. A plan you keep beats a perfect one you abandon.
  • Mixing subjects in a day helps memory more than doing one subject for hours.
