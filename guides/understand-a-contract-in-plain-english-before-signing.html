Understand a contract in plain English before signing

Get a plain-English summary of a contract: what you're committing to, unusual clauses and questions to raise before you sign.

Small Business AdminBeginner10 minAI assistant
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

Before you sign any supplier, lease or client agreement

Step by step

  1. Remove sensitive details

    Make a copy of the contract and remove bank details or personal information that isn't needed to understand the terms.

  2. Ask for a plain-English breakdown

    Upload the contract to your AI assistant with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyI'm a small business owner about to sign the attached [type of agreement] as the [customer / supplier / tenant]. In plain English, tell me: 1) what I'm agreeing to do and pay, 2) what the other side must do, 3) how long it lasts and how I can end it, including notice periods and fees, 4) automatic renewals, price rises or penalties, 5) who is liable if something goes wrong, 6) any clauses that seem unusual or one-sided, 7) 6 questions to ask before signing. Quote the clause number for each point. Don't tell me whether to sign.

  3. Check the key clauses yourself

    Read the clauses it quotes for notice, renewal, fees and liability in the actual contract.

  4. Get professional advice for anything important

    For high-value, long or unusual contracts, take the summary and your questions to a solicitor. AI can misread legal wording and doesn't know your full situation.

What you get

You agree to: pay £89/month for 36 months (clause 3.1). Ending early: you pay the remaining months in full (clause 9.2). Auto-renewal: renews for 12 months unless you give 90 days' notice (clause 10). Unusual: they can raise prices yearly with no cap (clause 4.4). Ask: 'Can we cap annual increases?'

Tips

  • A solicitor should check anything you're unsure about. This helps you ask better questions; it isn't legal advice.
