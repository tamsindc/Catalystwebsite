Write a reflective account for revalidation

A well-structured reflective account from your own notes, using a recognised model and linked to your professional standards, ready to edit.

NurseBeginner15 minAI assistantVoice notesWord
Open the interactive guideBrowse all guides

When to use it

After a learning moment, or when your revalidation is coming up

Step by step

  1. Capture what happened

    Record a quick voice note or jot down what happened, what you learned and what you'll do differently. Leave out names and anything that could identify a patient or colleague.

  2. Ask for a structured draft

    Paste your notes into your AI assistant with this prompt.

    Prompt to copyI'm a registered nurse preparing a reflective account for revalidation. Using my notes below, write a reflective account of about 300 words using [Gibbs' reflective cycle / What? So what? Now what?]. Cover: what the experience or learning was, what I learned from it, how it changed or will change my practice, and which themes of my professional standards it links to: [e.g. prioritise people, practise effectively, preserve safety, promote professionalism and trust]. Keep it in the first person and in my own plain style. Do not invent details I haven't given. My notes: [paste anonymised notes].

  3. Make it yours

    Read it through and rewrite anything that doesn't sound like you or isn't quite what happened. Your reflection must be your own honest account.

  4. File it

    Save it in Word with the date in your revalidation folder, and discuss it with your reflective discussion partner.

What you get

What happened: I attended a session on recognising sepsis early. What I learned: ... How it changed my practice: I now check the full set of observations before escalating... Standards themes: preserve safety; practise effectively.

Tips

  • Check the current requirements from your regulator, as forms and themes can change.
  • Doing one short account after each course is much easier than writing five the month before.
